Valuation Methodology

Three-Scenario Market Valuation

We ran a three-scenario analysis (Base, Bear, and Bull). Our Base Case (★) projects 2028 revenue at $3.0B. Applying a 13x EV/Revenue multiple yields a $39B Base Valuation. We added a $4B strategic premium for Blue Moon's HLS position. Total Base Case Enterprise Value: $42B.

Risk Factor Analysis

High

New Glenn Execution Risk

AI: Delays in operational launch cadence or early mission failures would severely impact Project Kuiper deployment revenue.

Rationale: Blue Origin’s $65B valuation relies entirely on the assumption that their heavy-lift rocket, New Glenn, will fly frequently and reliably starting in 2026/2027. Developing a reusable heavy-lift vehicle is incredibly difficult. If New Glenn suffers from prolonged launch delays, or worse, an early mission failure, the company will not be able to launch Amazon's Project Kuiper satellites on time.

Defense: If New Glenn does not achieve a steady launch cadence by 2027, our revenue model collapses. A failure here doesn't just mean a lost rocket; it means billions in deferred or canceled commercial contracts. This is exactly why we applied a discounted 15x multiple and recommended a HOLD instead of a BUY.

Solution: De-risk execution by prioritizing incremental validation: complete multiple successful test and early operational flights, reduce cadence ramp-up pressure, and diversify launch backlog beyond Project Kuiper to avoid single-program dependency during early operations.

Medium

Customer Concentration

AI: Heavy reliance on Amazon (Project Kuiper) and NASA for the vast majority of short-to-medium-term revenue.

Rationale: A healthy aerospace company has hundreds of diverse commercial and government clients. Right now, Blue Origin’s future revenue is dangerously concentrated in just two massive clients: Amazon (Project Kuiper) and NASA (Blue Moon).

Defense: Our medium risk is customer concentration. If Amazon pivots its satellite strategy, or if the U.S. Congress slashes NASA's Artemis budget, a massive percentage of our projected 2028 revenue disappears overnight. While both clients are currently highly secure, having so much revenue tied up in just two entities is a structural vulnerability.

Solution: The main risk to Blue Origin’s current strategy appears to be positioning itself in the growing Low Earth Orbit (LEO) economy. With LEO already saturated by major players, namely SpaceX and its Starlink constellation, there is both risk and opportunity for Blue Origin depending on how access to space-based infrastructure is managed.

A potential direction could involve leveraging partnerships with companies like Amazon and its cloud platform Amazon Web Services to deliver data and connectivity services globally. One idea is to provide free or low-cost internet access—especially in developing regions—while integrating it into a broader ecosystem where users pay for premium services, memberships, or enhanced data access.

In this model, basic connectivity could be free worldwide, enabling instant access to information, while revenue would come from higher-tier services, subscriptions, or enterprise-level cloud usage. This would expand global digital inclusion while creating a scalable business model built on data access and infrastructure rather than just connectivity alone.
Low

Space Tourism Saturation

AI: New Shepard suborbital tourism market experiencing demand saturation and margin compression.

Rationale: Suborbital space tourism (New Shepard) is a luxury market. The pool of individuals willing to pay hundreds of thousands of dollars for a 10-minute flight is fundamentally limited. As the initial hype fades, demand will plateau and margins will compress.

Defense: We ranked tourism saturation as a low risk not because it won't happen—it likely will—but because it doesn't matter as much to our macro valuation. Tourism only makes up 15% of our revenue model. Even if the suborbital tourism market completely dries up, the financial bedrock of the company (heavy launch and lunar contracts) remains fully intact.

Solution: Maintain tourism as a secondary revenue stream by optimizing costs, bundling experiences, and repositioning New Shepard for research, training, and institutional clients rather than relying purely on luxury demand.

Tie-Up to the Final Recommendations

Investors who ignore risk lose money. Our HOLD recommendation is a direct reflection of our High-Risk factor within the three main fields identified. We advise keeping our $500M in the bank until:

  • For New Glenn: until it proves it can mitigate its execution risk by completing three successful operational flights.
  • For customer concentration: until revenue diversification beyond Amazon and NASA is clearly established.
  • For suborbital space tourism: until demand stabilizes or the business successfully shifts toward alternative use cases.
